Website Blueprint for Samuel Kakraba, Ph.D.: Site Map, Section-by-Section Content Plan, and Build Recommendations
Dr. Kakraba should build a 10-item, research-first website on kakrabaresearchgroup.com: a personal "PI + lab" hybrid site organized around four research pillars, with SMART-Pred as the flagship. Global health data science in Ghana and Africa should run through every page as his distinctive brand, and a strong "Join the Lab" page should turn his record of mentoring 20+ international students into a recruiting asset. Privacy-sensitive, outdated and pre-faculty material belongs in an archive layer, not the main navigation.
TL;DR
	•	Structure: Home · About · Research · Publications · Software & Tools · Lab & Mentorship · Teaching · Talks & Media · Global Engagement · Join/Contact. CV download, service, awards and funding sit as sub-pages. This mirrors how leading AI-in-medicine and biostatistics labs (Zitnik Lab at Harvard, rafalab at Dana-Farber, Elizabeth Stuart at Johns Hopkins, Mukherjee Lab at Yale) organize their sites, and adds the Ghana/global-engagement and responsible-AI layers that set him apart.
	•	Positioning: "Explainable, accessible AI for public health — from molecules to populations, New Orleans to Accra." Lead with SMART-Pred (JMIR Aging case study, 91% test accuracy, a WSPH–CAIDS seed grant, Louisiana Department of Health collaboration). Next come verified metrics (17+ peer-reviewed papers, h-index 10, 3 patents/applications, 59 presentations, 47 verified reviews, 2 associate-editor roles) and the newest titles (CAIDS Senior Advisor; Associate Editor of JMIR Aging and Scientific Reports).
	•	Handle with care: don't name sponsored students or the 2016 surgery patient. Show only funded or awarded grants publicly (pending ones as "under review", without dollar amounts; leave unfunded ones off). Label manuscripts under review as preprints or "in review" only if a preprint exists. Move high school and primary school teaching and track and field to a brief "Story" note. Fix the inconsistencies (CAIDS's official name, Senior Advisor unit, citation counts, an outdated Tulane bio) before launch.
Key Findings from the Research
What best-practice guidance says.
	•	Jennifer van Alstyne (The Academic Designer, host of the Best Personal Academic Websites Contest) lists 35 possible page types, from Homepage, About/Bio, Awards, Service and Leadership to Research/Projects, Teaching, Team/Alumni/Mentoring, Join the Team, Community Engagement, Publications, Talks, Workshops, Consulting, Media Kit, News and Blog. She stresses that you don't need them all; "consider the people you hope to reach."[1]
	•	She recommends abstracts on publication and talk pages because they "help more people find your website when searching for related topics online."[1]
	•	She points out that Altmetric badges can be embedded.[1] Altmetric's own page confirms that "the free badges are only available to individual researchers for personal web pages."
	•	OpenScholar's analysis of faculty sites finds five recurring categories. The "About" category should include a formal biography and a downloadable CV.[2]
	•	OpenScholar also advises: "If your personal academic website will be your main web presence, make sure to link back to it from all the other places you are active."[2]
What exemplary sites actually do. I reviewed six sites directly (October 2026):
	•	Zitnik Lab (Harvard, AI in medicine): menu is About · Research · Publications · Members · Teaching & Conferences · Datasets · AI Models · [flagship tool pages] · News · Join Us. The homepage pairs an "Open Positions" button with a mission statement, three research-theme blocks and a dated news feed with inline [Paper] [Code] [Project website] links.[3] Its Join Us page gives separate instructions for PhD students, undergraduates and visitors. It is candid about email load: "she may not respond to all applicants. Do not take this personally! We do review all applications!"[4]
	•	rafalab (Irizarry, Dana-Farber/Harvard): Home · About · People · Research · Books · Software · Teaching. Its homepage is a clickable grid of figures from papers, plus listed grant numbers.[5]
	•	Elizabeth Stuart (Chair of Biostatistics, JHU): built on Hugo Academic, with a CV PDF directly in the menu, a curated software-guide page and talk slides as PDFs. It also has a talks section still labeled "(CONTENT TO BE UPDATED)", a cautionary example of stale content.[6]
	•	Mukherjee Lab (Yale): a Quarto site with Vision/Mission/Values statements, a Software page of R packages and Shiny apps, an Alumnae page, and a publications list scraped automatically from PubMed.[7][8]
	•	Nigam Shah (Stanford): free-to-use press photos plus ready-made Short and Long Biographies with metrics, effectively a media kit for event organizers.[9]
	•	Jeff Leek: a "Work With Me" link in the header and a "Products" section (Talks, Papers, Software, Data, Books, Guides, Courses, Blog).[10]
	•	Pattern: Software and Teaching get their own menu items on 5 of the 6 sites. Only the most recruitment-oriented lab (Zitnik) has a full Join page, and it is the most useful page for prospective students.

	•	Don't name the students he sponsored or the teen whose surgery he fundraised for, and don't post amounts. Describe the commitment in aggregate.
	•	List mentees only with consent; avoid publishing visa or immigration details for any individual.
	•	Funding:
	•	Show current awards publicly.

	•	Manuscripts: "15+ under review" and "15 in preparation" shouldn't appear as headline metrics. On the public site, list only peer-reviewed work and public preprints. In-review items without a preprint can be mentioned by topic on pillar pages.
	•	Early career: keep secondary and primary school teaching in Ghana (Wesley Girls' High School, 2009–2013, and earlier schools) as a single sentence in the "Story" section. It makes a compelling origin narrative but shouldn't be a CV-style list. Omit high school achievements.
	•	Teaching evaluations: "80–82% would recommend" at Tulane reads weaker than EKU's near-100% and invites comparison. Use qualitative quotes, or phrase it as "consistently strong student evaluations" with selected comments.

Technical and Platform Recommendations
	•	Recommended: Quarto (or Hugo Blox "Academic CV/Research Group"), hosted free on GitHub Pages or Netlify under kakrabaresearchgroup.com.
	•	Why Quarto: he works in R; Quarto renders R/Python notebooks as blog posts; Yale's Mukherjee Lab uses it with publications pulled automatically from PubMed.[7][8]
	•	Hugo Academic (formerly Wowchemy) is MIT-licensed. It offers BibTeX publication import, talks and slides, and a research-group template with team, news and contact blocks, and is used at Stanford, MIT and elsewhere.[30][31][32][33]
	•	Either keeps content in Markdown he owns. Disregard the vendor marketing claims such as "citations up 3×"; they are unverified testimonials.[32][34][35]
	•	No-code alternatives:
	•	Owlstown: built for academics; Semantic Scholar/BibTeX import; custom domain on the Pro tier at $6/month billed yearly ($8 billed monthly), per Owlstown's pricing page.
	•	Squarespace or Framer: the most polished design, but weak publication handling.
	•	WordPress with a scholarly plugin: flexible, but more maintenance and security work.
	•	Since he already has GitHub and student developers, a static site is the best long-term fit.
	•	Publication automation: a single BibTeX/ORCID-sourced file that feeds the publications page through a GitHub Action (ORCID or PubMed API), with a quarterly manual check. Embed free Altmetric badges.[22]
	•	SEO:
	•	Use Person schema (JSON-LD) on About with name, jobTitle, worksFor (Tulane), alumniOf, knowsAbout (biostatistics, explainable AI, public health surveillance) and sameAs links to ORCID, Google Scholar, LinkedIn, GitHub and Tulane profiles.[36][37][38]
	•	Give each publication its own page with an abstract. If PDFs are hosted, add Google Scholar citation meta tags: Google Scholar's Inclusion Guidelines for Webmasters require "at least three fields" (the title, the full name of at least the first author, and the year of publication), and PDFs are "processed as if they had no meta tags at all, unless they're linked from the corresponding HTML abstracts using citation_pdf_url."
	•	Use descriptive page titles ("Samuel Kakraba | Explainable AI for Public Health | Tulane").
	•	Submit a sitemap to Google Search Console.
	•	Accessibility: build to WCAG 2.1 AA, ideally 2.2 AA:
	•	alt text on all figures; captions or transcripts on videos; color contrast that meets the standard; keyboard navigation; text-based (not scanned) PDFs.[39][40]
	•	Tulane is private, so the DOJ's ADA Title II web rule doesn't directly apply. But WCAG 2.1 AA is the federal benchmark, and DOJ's interim final rule of April 20, 2026 moved the Title II deadlines back by a year: per Jackson Lewis, to April 26, 2027 for entities with populations of at least 50,000 and April 26, 2028 for smaller ones (most sources give the original large-entity deadline as April 24, 2026).
	•	Other: a mobile-first layout; light and dark mode; privacy-respecting analytics (Plausible or GA4) to see which pages students and media use; a custom 404 page; a "Last updated" stamp; HTTPS; and a backup of all content in the GitHub repository.
